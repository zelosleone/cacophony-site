Privacy

Last updated 11 October 2026

Cacophony has no account of its own, no analytics and no ads, and it sends nothing to its developer. It talks to Apple Music for you, and to a few other services only when they're needed or when you turn them on.

What stays on your computer

  • Your settings, your queue, the artwork it has shown and its logs are kept in the app's folders on your computer (Settings > About shows where). They aren't uploaded anywhere.
  • You sign in to Apple Music on Apple's own sign-in page, inside the app. Your Apple Account password goes to Apple only. Apple gives the app a key to your library, which is kept on your computer.

What goes where

  • Apple Music (Apple): everything about music: browsing, search, your library, playback and lyrics. These requests go straight from your computer to Apple, as they do from Apple's web player, and Apple's privacy policy applies to them.
  • LRCLIB (lrclib.net): when Apple Music has no lyrics for a song, its title, artist, album and length, to look up community lyrics. This is on unless you turn it off in Settings > Lyrics.
  • Discogs (discogs.com): the genres and styles you browse in Explore, and the records and covers it shows there. Nothing about who you are.
  • Last.fm and ListenBrainz: only if you connect them in Settings: the songs you listen to (title, artist, album and when), saved to your account there. You can disconnect them at any time.
  • Discord: only if you turn it on in Settings: the song you're playing goes to the Discord app on your computer, which shows it on your profile.
  • itch.io: once a day the app asks itch.io whether a newer version is out (turn this off in Settings > About). You buy and download Cacophony on itch.io, under its privacy policy.
  • This site (GitHub Pages): when the app's key to Apple Music is near its expiry, it fetches a new one from here.

Each of these services sees your IP address when the app connects to it, as with any connection on the internet.

Children

Cacophony isn't meant for children under 13. Apple Music has its own rules for younger listeners.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted on this page with its date.
